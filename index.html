01 — About

Biography

◑
HEADSHOT
assets/images/headshot.jpg

Blake Kubena is an American actor, voice artist and teacher of Shakespeare. Born in Texas, he is based in London.

On screen, Blake plays Sgt. Powell in Nuremberg (2025), James Vanderbilt's World War II drama for Sony Pictures Classics, alongside Russell Crowe, Rami Malek, Michael Shannon, Richard E. Grant, Leo Woodall and John Slattery. The film was an Official Selection at the Toronto, San Sebastián, Zurich and AFI film festivals.

He is also known to television audiences as Prince Askold in the sixth and final season of History's Vikings, available worldwide on Prime Video. Other screen work includes Ferris in After Anyuta, from NUDES: The Anthology, which earned him a Best Supporting Actor nomination at the North Europe International Film Festival.

As a voice artist, Blake's credits include EA's Star Wars Battlefront II, ADR for Direct Line's RoboCop commercial, an adidas promo for Major League Soccer, the Canadian voice of Rexona, and a corporate film for Zaha Hadid Architects' Bee'ah headquarters. He also did performance capture for nDreams' VR title Phantom: Covert Ops (2020).

Blake's stage work has a strong classical focus. He played the title role in Blackeyed Theatre's UK tour of The Strange Case of Dr. Jekyll & Mr. Hyde, and Malcolm and Dromio of Syracuse with The Pop-up Globe in Auckland and Sydney. As an Associate Artist of Heady Conduct, he played Angelo at the Rose Playhouse and Parolles at the Minack Theatre. Other credits include Malcolm for the Factory Theatre Company and Horatio at the Midsummer Scene Festival in Dubrovnik. In the US, he was an Equity company member of the Alabama Shakespeare Festival, playing Antipholus of Syracuse, Cassio and Buckingham, and worked with the Shakespeare Theatre of New Jersey.

Blake holds an MA with Distinction from the Royal Central School of Speech and Drama and a BFA, summa cum laude, from the University of Cincinnati College-Conservatory of Music. He teaches Shakespeare on the London Theatre Programme at the British American Drama Academy (BADA).
